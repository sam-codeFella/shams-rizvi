I was born in Bhopal. My dad worked for Hindustan Machine Tools, a government job that moved us around, so for a while home was the small hill town of Nainital.

*[Photo: childhood in Nainital]*
<!-- restore as: ![Childhood in Nainital](/images/story/nainital.jpg) -->

I was a curious kid. I wanted to know how the world worked, what other people saw in it, and why they didn't spend every waking hour on the things they loved. I couldn't imagine not doing that.

For me, the first of those things was football.

I played every chance I got. I represented my school in more matches than I can count, and went on to represent Mumbai at the under-14 and under-19 levels, and the Mumbai district side. Football taught me things no classroom did: how to lose, how to show up the next day anyway, and how a team becomes more than the players in it. Around the same time, I found robotics.

I wanted to understand every piece of a machine. Why does this motor turn that way? What happens if I change this sensor? Could I make it smarter? I spent hours making, breaking and remaking things, long before I knew the word "engineer".

*[Photo: me as a kid with my first robot]*
<!-- restore as: ![My first robot](/images/story/robotics-young.jpg) -->

I never really stopped. Years later, I was still making robots, just bigger and more ambitious ones.

*[Photo: me older, still doing robotics]*

Then there was music.

I taught myself, one song at a time, and before I graduated I was making my own short mixes. Music taught me that what you make isn't finished until it lands with the person on the other end. You can create a feeling, layer by layer, but only the listener decides if it works.

*[Photo: music days, a mix or a set]*
<!-- restore as: ![Music days](/images/story/music.jpg) -->

I went to the Pune Institute of Computer Technology to study computer science. While I was there, I interned at IBM, working on enterprise software and data infrastructure for Fortune 500 clients.

After graduating, I joined Barclays, working on omnichannel banking applications across customer systems and payments, in one of the most regulated environments there is. I sat with traders and relationship managers and learnt what software is really for: helping people make decisions they can trust.

Then I joined Concentric AI, an early-stage data security startup. We laid the groundwork for its data pipelines from scratch. They grew to ingest terabytes of data a day and flag active breaches and data residency issues in under 120 milliseconds. I carried the pager, fixed things at odd hours, and became a co-inventor on a US patent.

*[Photo: Concentric days]*
<!-- restore as: ![Concentric days](/images/story/concentric.jpg) -->

Finance had always pulled at me. So in 2025, with my best mates, I started KnowYourCompany.ai to create the research platform we wished we had for our own investing. I'm honoured to lead it as CEO.

I designed it end to end: the data pipelines, the search, the agents that watch the markets, and the answers that cite their sources. I also learnt the parts no one teaches engineers: selling, pricing and fundraising. I've had the privilege of working with clients who together manage over a billion dollars, with roughly that much being researched through the software we made.

*[Photo: the KnowYourCompany.ai team, or a demo]*
<!-- restore as: ![KnowYourCompany.ai](/images/story/kyc.jpg) -->

It also taught me the biggest lesson of my career.

> Value in and of itself is not commercially viable. It has to be easily consumed, verified and understood. Everything else is just a facade.

Looking back, football, robots and music were teaching me the same thing all along. Work with your team, understand the machine, and never stop thinking about the person on the other end.

I'm eternally grateful for the time I've spent with my friends, creating products with the technology this world has to offer, and learning from the people we made them for. These days I spend a lot of my time thinking about where AI will create real value, and where the world is heading next.

There's still so much to do, and I'm confident about the next decade.

Maybe I didn't become a professional footballer, but I found my team, and I still get to create things people use every day. I'm grateful to my parents, to my friends, and to the strangers on the internet who taught me so much of what I know.
